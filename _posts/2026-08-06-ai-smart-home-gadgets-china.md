Smart home gadgets used to mean "on a timer" or "controlled by an app." The current generation
coming out of China's smart home manufacturers is different — these devices use on-device AI to
actually interpret what's happening in a room, not just react to a schedule or a button press.
Here are five categories worth paying attention to.

## 1. AI Robot Vacuums With Real Obstacle Avoidance

The latest robot vacuums from brands like Roborock and Dreame use onboard cameras and AI object
recognition to identify and steer around cables, pet waste, and shoes — not just bump into them
like older models. Many also build a live 3D map of your home to plan cleaning routes room by
room.

**Why it's interesting:** Earlier robot vacuums got stuck on obstacles constantly, which is why a
lot of people gave up on them. AI-based obstacle recognition is the feature that actually made
"just let it run while I'm out" realistic.

**Who it's for:** Anyone who tried a robot vacuum years ago and got frustrated with it getting
stuck — this generation solves the exact problem that turned people off.

## 2. mmWave AI Presence Sensors

Sensors like Aqara's presence sensor line use mmWave radar plus an AI processing layer to detect
not just "someone is in the room" but where they are, whether they're sitting or standing, and how
many people are present — far more precise than the old PIR motion sensors that trigger on any
movement and time out awkwardly.

**Why it's interesting:** This is what makes automated lighting and climate control actually feel
smart instead of annoying — lights that stay on while you're sitting still reading, instead of
switching off every 60 seconds.

**Who it's for:** Anyone who has tried basic motion-sensor lighting and been irritated by it
turning off mid-use.

## 3. AI Video Doorbells With Person/Package Detection

Newer video doorbells filter notifications through an AI model that distinguishes between a
person, a delivery, a car, and a passing animal — instead of pinging your phone every time a leaf
blows past the camera.

**Why it's interesting:** Notification fatigue is the main reason people disable doorbell camera
alerts entirely. AI filtering is what makes it practical to actually leave them on.

**Who it's for:** Apartment and house owners who want package/visitor alerts without a flood of
false notifications.

## 4. AI Air Quality & Climate Monitors

Small sensor pucks that track CO2, humidity, PM2.5, and temperature, then use an AI model to
recognize patterns — like CO2 climbing during sleep — and suggest or automatically trigger
ventilation, rather than just displaying raw numbers on a screen.

**Why it's interesting:** Raw air quality numbers mean little to most people. The value is in the
device correlating the data over time and telling you plainly when something's actually worth
acting on.

**Who it's for:** People in newer, well-sealed apartments where ventilation isn't automatic, and
anyone who's noticed feeling groggy in a closed-up bedroom.

## 5. AI Smart Locks With Facial/Behavioral Recognition

Beyond fingerprint and PIN entry, newer smart locks add camera-based AI recognition that can tell
apart household members and flag unfamiliar faces lingering at the door, logging events with
context instead of just a raw access log.

**Why it's interesting:** Older smart locks were essentially electronic versions of a physical
key. This generation adds a layer of awareness — who's actually at the door and whether their
behavior looks normal.

**Who it's for:** Families who want keyless entry for kids without giving up visibility into who's
coming and going.
