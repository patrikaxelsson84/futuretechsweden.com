If you're new to buying gadgets directly from Chinese sellers, the checkout process itself is
simple — it's what happens *after* you pay that tends to raise questions. Here's a plain-language
rundown for buyers in Sweden and the rest of the EU.

## Do I have to pay customs and VAT on top of the price?

Usually no, not as a separate surprise bill. Since July 2021, EU rules require VAT to be charged on
all goods imported from outside the EU, regardless of value — the €22 VAT-free threshold that used
to exist is gone. In practice, AliExpress (and most large marketplaces selling into the EU) collect
that VAT for you **at checkout**, through a scheme called IOSS, for orders up to €150. That means
the price you pay at checkout already includes Swedish VAT (25% on most consumer electronics), and
you shouldn't be charged again on delivery.

For orders **above €150**, VAT and possibly import duty are handled at the border instead of at
checkout, and the courier (commonly PostNord in Sweden) may collect it — plus a small handling fee
— before the parcel is released to you. This is the main reason many experienced AliExpress buyers
keep individual orders under that €150 mark when possible.

*Tax rules can and do change — if you're placing a larger order, it's worth checking
[Tullverket](https://www.tullverket.se) (Swedish Customs) for the current thresholds before you
buy.*

## How long does shipping actually take?

It depends heavily on the shipping method chosen at checkout:

- **Standard/free shipping:** often 2-5 weeks, since it typically travels by a slower consolidated
  freight route.
- **AliExpress Standard Shipping / faster tracked options:** commonly 1-2 weeks to Sweden.
- **Premium/express options:** as little as a few days, at a noticeably higher shipping cost.

Every listing shows an estimated delivery window before you buy — factor in a few extra days
around Chinese holidays (Golden Week in early October, Chinese New Year in late January/February),
when processing slows down across almost all sellers at once.

## What if the item doesn't show up, or arrives wrong or broken?

AliExpress's Buyer Protection applies automatically to most orders: if a package doesn't arrive by
the guaranteed delivery date, or arrives damaged, not as described, or not at all, you can open a
dispute directly from your order page and request a refund or replacement. Keep this in mind:

- **Don't confirm delivery** in the app until you've actually checked the item — confirming closes
  the protection window.
- **Photograph/video the unboxing** if anything looks off. It's the single most useful thing you
  can do to support a dispute.
- Most disputes are resolved within a few days once opened; you generally don't need to ship
  anything back for smaller items — AliExpress often refunds without requiring a return.

## A few practical tips

- **Read the actual listing specs**, not just the title — sellers sometimes list multiple
  variants (color, storage size, plug type) on one listing page, and the default selection isn't
  always the one shown in the photos.
- **Check seller ratings and recent reviews**, not just the star average — a seller with 98%
  positive across 50,000 orders is a safer bet than one with 100% across 12.
- **Use a card or payment method with its own buyer protection** as a second layer, in addition to
  AliExpress's own.
