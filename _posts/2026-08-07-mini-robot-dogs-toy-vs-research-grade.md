any single listing.

## Toy-Tier (roughly $30–80)

This is what most "AI robot dog" listings on AliExpress actually are: remote or voice-controlled
electronic toys that walk, sit, and perform pre-programmed routines. Some genuinely do let you
write custom command sequences through an app.

**What you actually get:** Scripted movement, not real balance or navigation. These robots don't
sense terrain, avoid obstacles reliably, or adjust their gait to stay upright — they play back
motions. The "AI" in the listing title usually refers to voice command recognition, not autonomous
decision-making.

**Who it's for:** Kids, gift buyers, and anyone who wants a fun desk/shelf robot without a real
technical project attached to it.

## Research-Grade (roughly $2,500+)

This is where brands like Unitree operate. Their Go2 series uses real dynamic balancing — the kind
of control system that lets a four-legged robot get shoved, stumble, and recover its footing
without falling over, the same category of engineering used in university robotics labs. It
navigates with onboard sensors (LiDAR/depth cameras depending on configuration) rather than
following a scripted path, and exposes an SDK so you can actually program it — not just trigger
pre-set tricks.

**What you actually get:** Real autonomous locomotion, obstacle sensing, and a genuine development
platform if you want to code your own behaviors, at a price point that reflects actual robotics
hardware rather than a toy motor and a plastic shell.

**Who it's for:** Robotics hobbyists, students, and developers who want a real platform to build on
— not a purchase to make on impulse.

## Where's the middle ground?

Honestly — there mostly isn't one, at least not yet on the mainstream market. The category jumps
almost straight from "toy" to "research platform" with very little in between. A few smaller
robotics companies (Hengbot among them) are working on more affordable quadrupeds aimed at the gap,
but we couldn't find a listing we'd confidently recommend at time of writing — worth watching this
space rather than assuming a mid-tier option exists today.

## Which One Should You Actually Buy?

If you just want something fun that walks and reacts to voice commands, save your money and get
the toy-tier version — spending more won't get you meaningfully more intelligent behavior at the
next price step up. If you specifically want to learn robotics, program real locomotion, or you're
a student/hobbyist who'll actually use an SDK, the research-grade tier is the one worth the jump —
but go in knowing it's a serious purchase, not a gadget impulse buy.
